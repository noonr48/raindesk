# Series Bible — After the Last Rain

**Status:** working narrative bible · parallel animation continuity

## 1. Logline

Anna, a sheltered fifteen-year-old registrar in the protected rain city of Laptoon, is separated from the evacuation fleet when the city's ancient barrier shatters. With her father and the city's leaders missing, she escapes through the floodworks beside three adventurers she barely knows, travels inland to their home city, and eventually crosses the continent toward Bashu—the coastal refuge where her father promised to find her.

## 2. The story in one sentence

> The girl who once stood inside a sanctuary and welcomed migrants through its gate must learn how to live after becoming one of them.

## 3. Emotional spine

Anna initially understands safety as a sealed place:

- walls;
- a known climate;
- records that confirm who belongs;
- a father who returns through the rain;
- a city that can say, “You are safe now.”

Laptoon's collapse removes every part of that definition. The journey then rebuilds safety through relationships and temporary acts:

- Tate notices danger before Anna can understand it;
- Anna opens the route that saves Tate's group;
- Naomi preserves the names of the dead;
- Liroz gives the group physical stability;
- Rambus turns an impossible distance into passage;
- Harramius gives Anna work, identity and routine;
- the Little Blue Tree shows that a home can exist inside danger;
- desert communities receive help carried from places Anna has already lost;
- Bashu gives her a door of her own.

The final reunion works because Anna does not recover the old life. Edward enters the new one she has built.

## 4. Themes

### Migration rather than adventure tourism

The environments are not a collection of fantasy postcards. Each place changes what Anna believes home can be.

- **Laptoon:** home as enclosure and inherited certainty.
- **The Rain Throat:** home physically expelling her.
- **The passage and first sunlight:** freedom as frightening exposure.
- **Old Sactum:** a ruined place that still shelters people for one night.
- **Rambus:** trust across species and scale.
- **Harramius:** a place first treated as a waiting room that becomes lived-in.
- **Little Blue Tree:** community built within acknowledged danger.
- **The west road:** temporary homes, mutual obligation and chosen departure.
- **Bashu:** arrival without immediate reunion.

### Missing is not the same as dead

Anna's work with manifests gives emotional form to uncertainty. She refuses to convert the unaccounted-for into the dead simply because the record would be cleaner.

### Forced attachment versus chosen attachment

Laptoon attached itself to Hethrn through chains. Anna's later relationships work because people can leave.

- Tate can help Anna without becoming permanently responsible for her.
- Harramius can matter even though Anna later departs.
- Edward can return to Anna rather than restore her childhood.

### A home can be temporary and still be real

The story should not imply that only the final permanent residence counts. A room, a root bridge, a giant's shoulder, an overnight treehouse and a bowl prepared by someone who remembers her order can all be real forms of home.

## 5. World route and travel logic

### Laptoon and Bashu

Laptoon and Bashu are allied coastal cities with a specific emergency maritime arrangement. The evacuation fleet is a prepared state response, not an ordinary cheap transport option.

Coastal travel remains difficult because it requires:

- permission from multiple coastal authorities;
- protected charts and experienced crews;
- ships built for dangerous shallows and deep-water creatures;
- money or government sponsorship;
- harbour access that inland states do not possess.

This preserves the source logic that most Laptoon visitors prefer sea travel when they can obtain it, while a desert state such as Farvious still finds the forest crossing the most affordable route.

### Tate's expedition

The clean reconciliation is:

- a Farvious medical institution sponsored the expedition to Laptoon;
- Harramius guild adventurers were contracted as its travelling protection;
- Naomi and Liroz are Harramius natives;
- Tate is a northern traveller who uses Harramius as a base and accepts temporary contracts;
- most of the expedition is lost before or during the return.

### Why Harramius comes before Bashu

After the fleet is swept away, Anna lacks:

- a ship;
- money and luggage;
- an approved coastal route;
- wilderness experience;
- a guild identity recognised across borders;
- supplies suited to the desert and western road.

Tate's group can reach Harramius, where they have contacts, family, work and institutional recognition. Anna's choice is therefore not “abandon Bashu.” It is:

> survive the wrong direction long enough to choose the right road later.

### Why Anna later leaves Harramius

Harramius gives her the means to leave without making her departure emotionally trivial:

- guild licence;
- money;
- field experience;
- equipment;
- a westbound caravan;
- relationships that remain meaningful after separation.

Her first migration is forced. Her second is chosen.

## 6. Hethrn: function, history and psychology

### Public myth

Laptoon remembers Hethrn as its willing eternal saint. Murals and ceremonies present him as calm, beautiful and permanently devoted to the city.

### Working truth

Hethrn was a healer and defensive fighter during the original refugee crisis. He agreed to hold a protective field until morning so civilians could survive one catastrophic night.

The founders discovered that his power could be distributed through a circular system. Fear of losing protection became policy. The temporary act was made permanent.

The city then grew over the restraints:

- twenty founding oaths became twenty chains;
- roads and waterworks followed their radial paths;
- neighbourhoods surrounded the anchor stations;
- the circular city plan became sacred geometry;
- the person beneath the system became a symbol.

### Awakening

When the barrier fails, Hethrn's dispersed defensive power contracts back around him. The golden chains emerge because the city and the prison have become physically inseparable.

He does not awaken with a coherent strategic plan. His first behaviour is bodily and instinctive:

- breathe;
- remain standing;
- destroy immediate threats;
- orient toward the unresolved restraint in the forest;
- move away from the place that held him.

He may save people incidentally because the monster attacking them enters his reach. He does not become Laptoon's wall again.

### Visual contradiction

Hethrn's actual body is not saintly spectacle:

- tall and underfed;
- worn face and deep eye bags;
- long hair, partly braided;
- visible collarbones and ribs;
- unexpectedly strong shoulders, arms and back;
- frayed cloth and old medical wraps.

His magic creates the apparent demon:

- a faded skull behind the head;
- many elongated golden skeletal arms;
- arms bending around him like ribs;
- hands holding his shoulders, waist and neck as though the power is both guardian and restraint;
- chains passing between wrists and body.

## 7. Character arcs

### Anna

**Beginning:** intelligent but sheltered; understands other people's journeys through documents and stories.  
**Rupture:** loses city, route, belongings, sister-figure and father in one event.  
**First journey:** contributes city knowledge while depending on strangers for the world outside.  
**Harramius:** earns identity and competence rather than receiving a heroic destiny.  
**Second journey:** leads, contributes and chooses departure.  
**End:** receives Edward into a room containing evidence of several homes.

### Tate

Tate begins as a traveller Anna processes at the gate. She later becomes the first person to move toward Anna when the sheltered girl does not understand the incoming storm.

Her care is practical:

- pull Anna into shelter;
- read air and terrain;
- offer a bed;
- explain guild systems;
- share a route because it also serves her own life.

She is not converted into Anna's permanent guardian. Her ability to leave is part of what makes the relationship healthy.

### Naomi

Naomi gives the dead individual names. She carries grief without turning it into theatrical collapse and teaches Anna that remaining beside someone may be enough.

### Liroz

Liroz is the group's physical assurance and directness. Her compact body contrasts with extraordinary strength. She acts while others are still building theories and prevents the story from becoming uniformly solemn.

### Edward

Edward begins as the person who makes safety for Anna through warmth, food and patient listening. At the end, Anna repeats those actions for him.

### Zephrine

Zephrine connects Anna's old life to Bashu. Their separation at the harbour is physical and visible; their reunion confirms that the fleet survived even though the missing forest party did not arrive with it.

## 8. Recurring motifs

### Rain and water

- familiar Laptoon rain;
- horizontal storm after the sphere breaks;
- floodwater carrying Anna from home;
- the absence of rain at first sunlight;
- scarce water in the desert;
- rain beneath the Little Blue Tree;
- a sunlit coastal shower in Bashu.

### Doors, gates and thresholds

- Anna admits Tate's expedition;
- Laptoon's gate keeps the Kenwid outside;
- the floodgate sends the group away;
- the cave opens into sunlight;
- Harramius admits Anna with minimal paperwork;
- Anna works at Bashu's reception house;
- Edward arrives through a knock.

### Chains and roots

- dead golden roots tear Laptoon apart;
- living roots support Little Blue Tree;
- the contrast should be visible without explanatory dialogue.

### Documents and messages

- wet roof-line message;
- arrival paperwork;
- final manifest;
- Edward's note;
- expensive, uncertain letters;
- Bashu missing-person lists;
- a final knock that needs no written record.

### The warm drink

Edward makes milk, egg and sugar for Anna after the gate incident. Anna later makes it while waiting in Bashu and gives it to Edward when he returns.

## 9. Tone

The story can contain fear, loss and monsters without becoming a gore-first dark fantasy.

Priority order:

1. psychological disorientation;
2. sensory contrast;
3. scale and environmental consequence;
4. character choice;
5. implied violence;
6. explicit violence only where indispensable.

Wonder must remain genuine. The outside world is dangerous, but the story does not argue that Laptoon was right to seal Anna away forever.
