# Location Guide — After the Last Rain

**Purpose:** consolidate the manuscript's most visually specific places into production-ready environment references.  
**Rule:** source description first; adaptation interpretation second; generated images are references, not authority.

## 1. Laptoon — city behind the rain

### Source identity

Laptoon is an old cliffside city near the forests of Demish, bounded by forest and sea. Its weather is almost always dark and rainy. At approximately four in the afternoon the city can already look like night. Oil lamps provide much of the visible warmth. Moss covers building edges and fungi are deliberately cultivated on sections of homes; outsiders call it “the city where the garden is flipped to the side.”

### Spatial language

- narrow stone streets and crooked alleys;
- small wooden and stone houses close together;
- external walls treated as living garden surfaces;
- large outer walls and a ditch/gap around the gate;
- red-brick gate tunnel;
- circular city structure;
- sea and cliff always present as larger-scale geographic context;
- Tower of Gods on a steep hill slightly off-centre toward the sea.

### Shot library

**A. Exterior establishing:** high three-quarter view in heavy rain; dark cloud ceiling, amber lamps, dense wall fungi, sea glimpsed beyond the outer wall.

**B. Street-level domestic:** Anna walking through wet stone alleys; fungus gardens and roof-line messages visible behind her.

**C. Gate arrival:** red-brick tunnel, bridge dropping over the ditch, exhausted carts emerging from forest rain.

**D. Morning city:** same streets brighter; vendors open shutters, lamps and steam-food stalls create a more social rhythm.

**E. Collapse:** same geography under horizontal wind, buildings lifting, golden chains pulling from beneath streets, sea entering the city.

### Visual principle

Laptoon must feel desirable before it becomes tragic. The audience should understand why someone would call it home.

## 2. Laptoon gate complex

### Source identity

The main entrance uses a large stone wall, heavy wooden gates reinforced with steel, and a brick-lined tunnel. The gate itself can lower to form a bridge across the surrounding ditch. It is a checkpoint, evacuation control point and psychological boundary.

### Important recurring details

- watch stations above the wall;
- paired operating wheels;
- red-purple signal whistle;
- guard booths and paperwork;
- medical route immediately inside;
- rain suddenly becoming quieter once the door is closed.

### Animation use

This is the physical representation of Anna's childhood definition of safety. Use the same gate in the opening, the Kenwid incident, evacuation scenes and finally the city's fall.

---

## 3. Tower of Gods

### Source identity

The largest structure in the city. It sits slightly off-centre, closer to the sea, on top of a steep hill. Large walls surround the hill and a small village also occupies the elevated ground. High officials, nobles and church leadership use the tower.

### Interior/council level

The fourth floor contains a large circular room used for political meetings. A coffin symbolising Hethrn's sacrifice is suspended in the centre and lit from around it rather than by a conventional lamp.

### Roof garden / Hethrn chamber

The stairway changes from expensive marble to worn stone near the top. A simple rectangular door opens onto the roof:

- circular stone pond;
- loose bronze cagework shaped like tree roots;
- small circular stepping platforms above still water;
- central grass island;
- kneeling hero statue with sword planted into grass;
- strong wind around the perimeter;
- surprisingly warm, calm air inside the central garden.

The source says the land was once believed to be sun-loving and rainless, which helps explain why the founding church was placed there.

### Camera language

**Approach:** narrow staircase becoming rougher as the audience ascends.  
**Reveal:** door opens into bright cold sky and moving water.  
**Hero shot:** low camera from stepping stones toward Hethrn.  
**Emotional shot:** Anna crosses the invisible calm boundary into the warm grass.

---

## 4. The bronze protective circle

A large carved bronze boundary/line surrounds Laptoon. It is repeatedly treated as the place travellers dream of reaching because crossing it means they have entered the protected zone.

### Visual function

Do not make it merely a glowing forcefield. It should feel like an old monumental civil structure embedded into roads, gates, stones and landscape. The later glass sphere is the expanded manifestation of the same protective system.

### Collapse cue

The sphere above the city cracks like glass. The bronze boundary and buried gold chains then give the audience the sense that a physical system has failed, rather than a generic magic shield being turned off.

---

## 5. Forest outside Laptoon

The forest is not generic dark fantasy woodland. It is a wet, densely alive ecosystem containing many strange creatures and plants. It is dangerous enough that ordinary travellers rarely cross it and coastal travel is generally preferred when available.

### Recurring visual elements

- enormous trunks;
- storm-fed streams;
- glowy eyes hidden in plants;
- furred caterpillar-like creatures with bird heads;
- rainbow/star-patterned mushrooms;
- skull, tube and lip-shaped fungal forms;
- abandoned carts and expedition debris in dangerous regions.

The forest should remain visually beautiful even when it is lethal.

---

## 6. Forest of Keltons

A specifically described dangerous sub-region. Plants look white-green and soft, almost like cotton. On closer inspection this appearance comes from enormous webs made by highly poisonous creatures.

### Visual progression

1. peaceful pale-green forest;
2. cotton-like surfaces on branches;
3. realisation that the “cotton” is webbing;
4. masks go on;
5. a white-furred spider attacks from above;
6. the party exits back into ordinary green forest.

The source mentions a historical expedition of forty disappearing here, so abandoned traces can be used sparingly.

---

## 7. Old Sactum

### Source identity

A fallen city from the war period, formerly a major consumer and partner of Harramius. It sits on top of an enormous tree stump the size of a hill.

A narrow carved road/stair wraps around the trunk and takes roughly half an hour to climb.

### Arrival view

At sunset the party reaches:

- worn stone castle;
- broken towering walls around the perimeter;
- scattered fallen masonry;
- long-abandoned gatehouse;
- remnants of lamp fuel hidden in heavy bark.

### Animation use

The location should not become a gothic dungeon spectacle. Its emotional function is simpler: a dead city still provides shelter for one night to people who have just lost their living city.

---

## 8. River route and the giant fish

After leaving the first hidden passage, the group follows a river. A spare small boat is swallowed by an enormous one-eyed river creature, immediately establishing that larger water is not automatically safer than land.

### Shot

Wide river curve, tiny boats, enormous shadow below surface, one single movement, boat gone. Avoid over-explaining the creature.

---

## 9. Ancient stone water-face / Blue Angel's Cry region

### Source identity

A large carved woman's face is embedded in stone beside the river. The carving is supported by natural brown rocky steps and partly covered by dark blue-green vegetation.

Distinct details:

- face tinted blue;
- moss matching the surrounding colour;
- hollow eyes;
- empty nose openings;
- slightly open mouth;
- green-blue bubbling water escaping from the face;
- water matches the river;
- mellow blue mist from algae in the water.

### Adaptation use

This becomes the visual outfall of the Rain Throat. The group should emerge from the mouth of the carved face in a small maintenance skiff and immediately realise they are outside Laptoon.

### Perspective options

- river-level wide shot with the face above;
- close low-angle looking into the mouth waterfall;
- reverse shot from behind the face as the boat emerges into daylight.

---

## 10. Harramius

### Source identity

An inland/desert city whose history is tied strongly to mineral processing. The country is surrounded by sand and actively wants new residents. The source describes four historical rings, a diverted river, broad city transport and a view toward the open sea.

### Urban character

- pale warm stone;
- practical mineral and metal construction;
- broad commercial roads;
- crowded food streets;
- transport carts and beasts;
- river routes crossing the city;
- old crumbling areas around the edges;
- strong contrast between central activity and abandoned former neighbourhoods.

### Emotional use

Anna initially sees Harramius as too bright, too dry and too crowded. The city slowly becomes familiar through routine: breakfast, markets, guild halls, Faze's home, dungeon trips and vendors recognising her.

### Design principle

Use subtle desert/North-African rhythm in fabrics, shade and geometry without turning the city into an Egyptian theme park. It is a multicultural trade city.

---

## 11. Harramius guild hall

The guild hall has a large central room with a five-metre hourglass and a busy but impersonal atmosphere. Its architecture should communicate bureaucracy, contracts and information rather than fantasy tavern clichés.

### Useful shots

- Anna dwarfed beneath the hourglass;
- Gil at the evaluation desk;
- contract boards and guild counters;
- equipment/material exchanges;
- Anna leaving with a new identity and practical work.

---

## 12. Harramius outskirts and old rings

The source distinguishes the busy current city from older crumbling structures. The outskirts can show the historical layers of a city that has repeatedly expanded and abandoned itself.

### Visual rhythm

new market → old wall → abandoned road → mineral yard → homes → open desert.

This lets the city feel old without relying on exposition.

---

## 13. Dungeon approach

The Harramius dungeon is large enough to contain an established economy near the entrance and dangerous levels farther below.

### Visual language

- deep red tunnels;
- mana-lit stone;
- shops and material traders near accessible levels;
- increasingly strange flora and monsters farther down;
- biological specimens that Anna can identify;
- practical equipment rather than treasure-pile fantasy.

The dungeon is an economic and ecological system, not merely a battle arena.

---

## 14. Little Blue Tree

### Source identity

A cavern on the third stone layer of the Harramius dungeon. The room extends at least roughly one kilometre down. An enormous upside-down oak-like tree attaches to the ceiling.

The roots are so large that one can function as a bridge. The tree's downward growth reaches only partway toward the floor while branches and leaves hang in open space.

Additional features:

- crystal-like blue dome overhead;
- endless blue tint;
- soft indoor rain;
- clouds and mist beneath the tree;
- transparent bubbles rising from dark water;
- artificial platforms and buildings attached to the tree;
- workshops, homes and markets.

### Emotional visual match with Laptoon

The same ingredients recur:

- rain;
- living growth;
- vertical settlement;
- soft ambient water;

but here the structure is alive and shared rather than a prison built over a bound person.

### Camera language

Start from Anna's eye level. Let her move toward the edge of the root bridge. Only then reveal the kilometre-deep void.

## 15. Kameripa

### Source identity

A forest/mountain settlement. People live in stone homes near brown rocky mountains behind wooden fences.

The source describes the population as:

- heavier in fur;
- broader in body;
- shorter in stature;
- steel-like bones;
- larger teeth and wider mouths;
- stronger digestion;
- less effective communication with outsiders.

### Gate

The village gate is made from whole tree trunks tied together with roots and forest vines. The gatekeeper wears a steel-forged hat edged with leather, torn animal skins and rock-centipede scales, and carries a huge iron axe.

### Environmental identity

The settlement should feel built from the forest itself rather than imported into it. Smoke, raw food preparation, heavy wood and stone dominate.

---

## 16. Westbound red grasslands

These are currently an adaptation expansion grounded in the manuscript's relic-scale objects and travel landscape.

### Visual anchors

- red grass moving like a sea;
- giant swords and weapons embedded in the surface;
- enormous unidentified objects used as landmarks;
- caravans tiny against the horizon;
- occasional sudden weather walls.

The objects should never become a random “giant weapon field.” Their scale implies a forgotten history and assists navigation.

---

## 17. Tree refuge above desert monsters

Adaptation location for the second migration.

### Required image

A temporary settlement high inside a giant dry tree. The audience sees enormous monsters moving through the sand far below.

### Function

The danger exists underneath ordinary life. People cook, repair straps, roll sleeping mats and whisper while something huge repeatedly brushes the roots.

---

## 18. Desert sinkhole community

Adaptation location used to show scarce water and practical mutual dependence.

### Structure

- large circular sand sink;
- homes cut into the walls;
- rope lifts and ladders;
- protected water source at the bottom;
- windbreaks and shade cloth;
- communal filtration/repair areas.

### Story function

Anna contributes knowledge imported from Laptoon and Harramius rather than being a passive visitor. The community reciprocates by repairing her clothing and sharing practical local knowledge.

---

## 19. Bashu

The manuscript establishes Bashu as Anna's intended coastal destination and a major port of refuge, but the exact cityscape is less exhaustively described than Laptoon, Harramius and Little Blue Tree.

### Adaptation target

Bashu should therefore remain visually distinct but deliberately less over-specified until the source is expanded:

- bright coastal light;
- large harbour;
- reception house for displaced Laptoon residents;
- practical temporary housing;
- ships and caravans arriving together;
- visible evidence that many refugees made it here even though Anna's father did not arrive with the fleet.

The emotional emphasis is not architectural spectacle. It is a city where Anna finally has a door of her own.

---

## 20. Location staging rule

For each environment shot, construct three simultaneous layers:

### Foreground
Current emotional character/action.

### Middle ground
One ordinary human/social routine.

### Background
One impossible or fantastical environmental event.

Example: Anna checks a manifest in foreground; medical workers load supplies in middle ground; an enormous golden chain tears a street upward in background.

This is the preferred way to make the world feel alive without expanding the cast unnecessarily.
