# After the Last Rain — animation adaptation packet

**Status:** owner-directed working development · 2026-08-12 · parallel animation project  
**Primary source:** `Side story.pdf` (124 pages; source binary identified in the provenance packet)  
**Relationship to Where Nothing Falls:** separate continuity unless the owner later imports a specific idea

This folder consolidates the current animation work developed from the older side-story manuscript during the August 2026 MiniMax H3 sessions.

The project is not a rewrite of the live **Where Nothing Falls** game documents. It is a self-contained anime adaptation of the older **Laptoon / Eris** material. The protagonist is called **Anna** in the adaptation, but she is the character called **Eris** in the source PDF. Do not silently merge this Anna, Laptoon, Hethrn, Tate, Harramius or Bashu into the live sky-city canon.

## Current premise

> A sheltered fifteen-year-old who once admitted desperate travellers into a protected rain city becomes a refugee when that city collapses. Separated from the evacuation fleet and from her father, she crosses a continent with three strangers, builds a temporary life in an inland city, and eventually reaches the coastal city where her father promised to find her.

The story is about migration, the loss of home, temporary refuge, chosen attachment and the difference between preserving a place and preserving the people who lived there.

## Production intent

- Refined detailed anime, not a literal comic-panel adaptation.
- Multiple parts are allowed; the journey must retain scale and emotional breathing room.
- H3 shots should be designed as controlled short units with strong first/last-frame continuity rather than one long generation.
- The fall of Laptoon is the inciting rupture, not the complete story.
- Harramius must feel like a meaningful temporary home rather than a detour to be hurried through.
- The final reunion works because Anna has built a life capable of receiving Edward, not because the old home is restored.
- Novel descriptions take priority over broad real-world cultural shorthand in visual generation.
