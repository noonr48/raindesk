# Decision Log — After the Last Rain

**Purpose:** distinguish owner-locked adaptation decisions from proposals, historical source material and unresolved work.  
**Latest checkpoint:** 2026-08-12

## A. Owner-locked or strongly preferred direction

### Format and focus

- Adapt the side story as a polished refined-anime animation rather than attempt a literal book transcription.
- Multiple parts are acceptable. The story may run substantially longer than ten minutes if that is required to preserve the migration arc.
- The fall of Laptoon must have emotional weight; it cannot be treated as a brief spectacle before an unrelated adventure.
- The core story is migration: losing home, travelling across a large landscape, surviving through temporary communities and eventually finding a place to live again.
- The inspiration from refugee experiences should remain in the emotional structure and practical consequences, not be reduced to a slogan or a direct one-to-one real-world allegory.

### Anna

- Adapt source-character **Eris** as **Anna**.
- Lock Anna's age at **15**.
- Anna has **two arms and two eyes** in the adaptation.
- Preserve her blue hair, emerald/green eyes, dark green pointed ears, red wolf-emblem hat, oversized weather coat, analytical mind and sheltered upbringing.
- She must read visually and behaviourally as a teenager, not a mature adult adventurer.
- The current Anna reference is a strong visual anchor. Clothing is still a live art pass and should remain fantasy-first.

### Tate and the travelling group

- Tate is the character called **Shirley / Tat** in the source.
- Preserve the source appearance: roughly 16, pale/light skin, loose grey-to-white hair that is later explicitly long white hair, blue crystal-like eyes, long white coat with gold lining, black undershirt, one-handed sword and staff.
- Tate should not inherit Harramius visual culture merely because Harramius is her base.
- Harramius is Tate's adventuring base, not necessarily her homeland.
- Use the current working direction that Tate comes from a northern snow region and later accompanies Anna toward Bashu because the route also serves Tate's own travel northward.
- Naomi and Liroz are Harramius sisters; they return home there after the Laptoon expedition.

### Narrative cuts and tone

- Remove the fake-father / thirteen-hearts plot from this adaptation.
- Do not strand Anna because she impulsively rescues a new child or runs onto an exposed, tilting gangway.
- Reduce explicit gore. Psychological unease, environmental scale and implication are more important than severed bodies or detailed corpse displays.
- The old corpse tunnel may survive as atmospheric evidence of exclusion, erased names, mineral impressions and abandoned belongings, but it is not a gore showcase.
- Monsters remain consequential and frightening; the story is not sanitised into harmless fantasy.

### Hethrn

- Hethrn was used as a sacrifice to keep Laptoon safe and did not want an eternal role.
- His awakening must visually resemble a resurrection and an unbinding.
- Enormous golden chains emerge from beneath the circular city like roots, pulling streets and buildings because Laptoon was constructed over his restraints.
- Hethrn appears exhausted, malnourished and older than an idealised anime hero, yet unexpectedly sinewy and muscular.
- His power should look demonic despite the city's saintly image of him.
- Do not use a fully visible city-sized skeleton as the default manifestation. Current target: a faded partial skull plus many elongated golden skeletal arms, around two to three times Hethrn's scale, curving around him like a living rib cage. Some hands brace his shoulders, waist or neck; others float or attack.
- Hethrn attacks monsters entering his immediate reach or blocking his path, often on pure instinct. He does not resume his role as Laptoon's guardian.
- He heads into the forest toward the unresolved event involving the king, Edward, Anita, Edgar and the old binding mechanism.

### Laptoon's collapse

- The barrier is a literal sphere that cracks and shatters like glass.
- Its failure reveals that Laptoon's familiar rain and wind were softened interior conditions. The true exterior climate enters violently.
- Anna is completing the final manifest while several people remain unaccounted for. Her belongings are already aboard the ship.
- Tate's group approaches; the first crack appears; Naomi and Liroz shelter in a nearby building.
- Tate reacts faster than Anna, rushes forward and pulls Anna from the exposed dock into the building before the full wind front arrives.
- The ship is physically swept away by wind, surge and broken moorings. It is not a captain choosing to abandon Anna.
- Monsters begin entering while houses, pavements and buried chains tear loose.
- Water floods the harbour building. The group asks Anna for another way out.
- Anna leads them down to an old flood-control tunnel containing small maintenance boats. Her scan identifies three approaching monsters; the group launches immediately.

### Route and destination logic

- **Bashu remains Edward's promised meeting destination.** Edward has no reason to tell Anna to meet him in Harramius.
- Laptoon and Bashu are coastal partners with a special evacuation route. This is not a universally available ferry service.
- Sea travel requires permits, protected charts, political access and exposure to deep-water danger. Inland Farvious/Harramius travellers therefore still have reason to cross the forest.
- Harramius is the survivable detour: Tate's group knows the route and has guild contacts, family, equipment and shelter there.
- Anna goes to Harramius to survive, earn money, obtain a recognised guild identity, train, send messages and prepare for the westward journey.
- Harramius must remain a developed setting and genuine temporary home.
- Anna later leaves Harramius by choice and continues to Bashu with a protected caravan.
- The final reunion occurs in Bashu after Anna has arrived first, searched without success and begun building a life. Edward eventually knocks on her door.

### Visual culture

- Refined detailed anime is the target.
- Laptoon may carry a rainy western/English-like atmosphere, but not literal historical English costume or architecture.
- Harramius may carry a subtle desert/North-African/Egyptian-like rhythm, but not direct pharaonic cosplay or museum jewellery.
- Novel descriptions take priority over broad real-world cultural shorthand.
- Clothing should be fantasy, attractive and practical, with modest climate-appropriate exposure rather than either total concealment or extreme fan-service.
- Anna and Tate are adolescents; attractiveness comes from face, expression, acting, silhouette and clothing design rather than mature sexualisation.

## B. Strong working directions, still reviewable

- Working title: **After the Last Rain**.
- Eight-part structure: *The City Behind Rain; The Last Rain; The Wrong Direction; The Giant Road; A City Without Rain; Rain Beneath the Earth; The Road West; The Knock*.
- Hethrn originally agreed to protect refugees for one night; the founders made the sacrifice permanent.
- Twenty founding oaths became twenty golden chains and defined Laptoon's circular plan.
- The first king's enormous spear functions as a master restraint or unresolved key in the forest.
- Hethrn's defensive manifestation was stretched into the barrier; the barrier's collapse causes it to contract back around him.
- The flood route's common working name is **the Rain Throat**.
- Tate accompanies Anna west under a paid escort contract and later separates toward her northern homeland.
- The Little Blue Tree is the emotional answer to Laptoon: living roots support a community, whereas dead golden “roots” imprisoned a person.
- The blue carved stone face beside the river is the preferred Rain Throat outfall because its source description is unusually distinctive and visually useful.

## C. Current location-design canon for visualisation

The source-grounded environment set now includes:

- Laptoon's rain-soaked stone streets, vertical fungus gardens, outer walls, gate tunnel and sea/cliff setting.
- The Tower of Gods and its roof garden: circular pond, bronze root-like cage, stepping stones, central grass and Hethrn's kneeling statue.
- The bronze protective circle and the glass-like spherical barrier above the city.
- The Forest of Keltons and its white-green webbed environment.
- Old Sactum on the giant tree stump with its narrow carved ascent and ruined castle gatehouse.
- The blue stone woman's face beside the river, leaking green-blue water and surrounded by mellow blue algae mist.
- Harramius as a four-ring mineral-trade city with diverted river, markets, transport, old outskirts and open sea views.
- The Harramius guild hall and dungeon entrance/economy.
- Little Blue Tree: kilometre-scale inverted tree, crystal dome, indoor rain, clouds, bubbles, roots used as bridges and platforms, and a functioning underground settlement.
- Kameripa: forest/mountain stone settlement behind wooden fencing, heavy-furred inhabitants and root-bound gate construction.
- The western red grasslands, relic-scale swords, tree refuge above monsters and desert sinkhole community as adaptation travel environments.
- Bashu as a coastal refugee destination whose exact architecture remains less specified than Laptoon/Harramius/Little Blue Tree.

The dedicated source-grounded environment document is now `LOCATION_GUIDE.md`. Generated scenic plates are exploratory references unless promoted separately.

## D. Explicitly rejected or superseded

- Four-armed or four-eyed Anna in the animation.
- Four-eyed Zephrine in the animation; retain the sense of her unusual ancestry through horns, eyes and physical ability without cluttering the face.
- Fake Edward as a central plotline.
- Anna running onto an exposed gangway after the barrier breaks.
- Anna missing the ship because she adopts or escorts a newly introduced child.
- Hethrn conveniently defending the entire city and making the evacuation easy.
- Hethrn immediately finding a peaceful cottage as the emotional resolution of his awakening.
- A clean heroic saint design for Hethrn.
- A fully explicit, giant anatomical skeleton dominating every shot.
- Corpse-cave gore as the primary identity of the underground passage.
- Over-literal English and Egyptian costuming.
- Treating every generated character sheet as authoritative; several are superseded exploration.
